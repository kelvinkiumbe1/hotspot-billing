Attribution is not required by the Unsplash Licence. It is recorded anyway
because the question that matters later is not "must we credit this" but "are
we allowed to be using it at all", and the only way to answer that is to have
written down where it came from at the time.

## The unverified ones

`customer.jpg` shows an identifiable person and is used to advertise a paid
service. The Unsplash Licence — if that is even where it came from — covers the
photographer's copyright and explicitly does **not** grant rights to people
depicted in a photograph; a model release is a separate thing and is not
guaranteed. Of everything here that is the image most likely to draw a
complaint, and nobody currently knows its origin.

Either establish where it came from, or replace it with a photograph taken by
the operator of somebody who has agreed to appear. It renders only above the
`md` breakpoint, so it is absent from the phones most customers use, and
dropping it costs very little.

The two login backgrounds are the same question with far lower stakes: no people
in them, and they are seen only by staff.

## Replacing the hero

`hero-city.webp` is a placeholder with a shelf life. A photograph of the
operator's own street, tower or shopfront beats any stock skyline, because a
customer recognising where they are is worth more than a well-composed city they
have never seen. It is what the per-operator hero upload is for.

If you do swap it: 2000px wide is enough for the widest the hero ever renders,
WebP at quality 82 lands around 115 KB, and the left third wants to stay
uncluttered — the headline sits there over a dark gradient.
